Mounted volumes and their space. `list` shows each volume with total, used and free space and whether it is removable; `usage` answers for the volume holding one path (your home folder by default). *Free* is what you can still write, the same number Finder, Explorer and `df` show as available. `eject` unmounts and ejects a removable drive (`diskutil` on macOS, `udisksctl` on Linux, the same calls Explorer uses on Windows) and refuses the system volume.

`top` answers "what is filling my disk": it ranks the biggest folders (and files with `--files`) under a path, your home folder by default. The walk runs in parallel, never follows symlinks, stays on one volume and stops at `--timeout` (5 s by default); a walk cut short says `partial: true` and its sizes are lower bounds. Sizes are allocated bytes on macOS and Linux (what the files really take on disk) and apparent bytes on Windows. Well-known places carry a tag, a note and a `safe` verdict (`yes`, `review`, `tool` or `no`): `trash`, `downloads`, `cache` (`~/Library/Caches`, `~/.cache`, `%TEMP%`), `temp`, `xcode-derived-data`, `docker`, `ios-backup`, `spotlight-index` and `node_modules`. It only reads; delete with `aos file delete`.
